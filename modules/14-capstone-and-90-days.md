# Module 14: Capstone and your first 90 days as an AI-native design lead

> **Time:** about 8 hours · **Output:** a complete portfolio case study, a 20-minute presentation, and a 90-day plan for your new role

---

## Learning objectives

- Assemble your capstone into a **compelling lead-level case study**.
- Present it to a **mixed audience** (design, engineering, leadership).
- Turn everything into a concrete **90-day plan** for your new role.
- Set up habits to **keep learning** as tools change.

### The case-study narrative

Lead-level case studies show **judgment, systems and impact**, not just screens. Structure:

1. **Context:** the product area, users and why it matters.
2. **The challenge:** user problem *plus* the organisational and AI-era challenge (for example "UI generated by many people and agents, inconsistently").
3. **Approach:** your workflow, and where AI was and wasn't used, *and why*.
4. **Research and validation:** real users, what the interactive prototype revealed, iteration.
5. **The system:** tokens, components, AI-readiness, and how standards reach agents.
6. **Context engineering:** UX.md, plus experiment results (before/after scores and visuals).
7. **Design to code:** the MCP loop, drift log insights, and rule improvements with evidence.
8. **Collaboration and leadership:** contract, policy, pilot plan.
9. **Outcomes and metrics:** what improved, with numbers from your logs and experiments.
10. **Reflection:** what you'd do differently, what you chose *not* to automate, and what's next.

**Show evidence everywhere:** AI log stats, rubric scores, drift reduction, test findings.

### Quality bar

Before presenting, check each item:

- [ ] Every claim is backed by an artifact or data
- [ ] AI's role is transparent (PACED applied to your own case study)
- [ ] The human decisions are visible: what you chose, rejected and why
- [ ] Accessibility is evident throughout
- [ ] It can be understood in 5 minutes (summary) *and* explored in depth

## Part B: The presentation (20 minutes)

| Minutes | Section |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | The problem and why AI changes the stakes |
| 2–6 | Research and the prototype: what interactivity revealed |
| 6–11 | The system: tokens → AI-ready → context (show the experiment) |
| 11–15 | Design to code live or recorded: Figma frame → agent → running UI → QA |
| 15–18 | Leadership: policy, collaboration contract, pilot |
| 18–20 | Outcomes, reflection, ask or next steps |

**A live demo beats slides.** If you can, show the MCP loop live with a pre-recorded fallback. Watching an agent build from *your* system with *your* rules is a powerful moment for an engineering or leadership audience.

**Rehearse with three audiences in mind:**

- **Designers:** "How do I work this way?"
- **Engineers:** "Will this make my job easier or harder?"
- **Executives:** "What's the value and risk?"

## Part C: Your first 90 days as lead

Use this as a template. Adapt it after your first weeks of listening. Template: [templates/90-day-plan.md](../templates/90-day-plan.md).

### Days 1–30: Listen and baseline

**Goal:** understand before changing.

- 1:1s with every designer, the eng lead, PMs, research, and the DS owners (if any).
  - What's working? What's painful? How do you use AI today? What worries you?
- **Audit:**
  - Design system: run the **AI-readiness scorecard** (Module 08).
  - Workflow: map the current process and where AI is (unofficially) used.
  - Tools and data: what's being used? Any data risks?
  - Design–eng: how does handoff work? Where does UI drift?
- **Baseline metrics:** time to first user test, design-QA defects, test frequency, team sentiment.
- **Quick win:** pick one painful, low-risk thing (for example busywork in Figma) and help fix it.
- **Don't:** announce big AI changes, buy tools, or rewrite the system.

**Output:** a "State of design and AI" memo (2 pages) shared with your manager and the team.

### Days 31–60: Foundations and a pilot

**Goal:** establish guardrails and prove value on a small scale.

- **Policy v1:** draft with the team; review with security and legal.
- **Collaboration contract v1:** co-write with the eng lead.
- **System foundations:** fix the biggest AI-readiness gaps (often naming, variables and descriptions for the top 10 components).
- **Context v1:** DESIGN.md and a first UX.md from existing research.
- **Pilot:** one team, 4–6 weeks, with hypotheses and the baseline.
- **Training:** start the team version of this course (Modules 01–03).

**Output:** policy v1, contract v1, pilot launched, system improvements shipped.

### Days 61–90: Evidence and roadmap

**Goal:** show results and set direction.

- **Pilot review:** metrics against baseline, plus stories. Scale, adjust or stop.
- **Engineering partnership:** plan the token pipeline and Code Connect for the top components.
- **Rituals:** drift review, DS sync, prototype showcase (Module 11).
- **Roadmap (6–12 months):** system, context, tooling, skills, rituals.
- **Executive update:** the one-slider (Module 13).

**Output:** a pilot report, a 6–12 month roadmap and an executive update.

### Risks to watch

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Moving too fast and alienating the team | Listen first; opt-in pilots |
| Being seen as "the AI person" rather than the design lead | Lead with quality and users; AI is a means |
| Engineering sees design as overstepping | Co-own the contract; ask before touching code |
| Leadership expects magic | Evidence-based expectations; publish the pilot results |
| System work stalls under feature pressure | Tie system work to pilot outcomes and to the drift data |

## Part D: Staying current without drowning

Tools change monthly, so build a sustainable learning system:

- **Weekly (30 min):** skim one trusted source, such as the [NN/G AI topic](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/ai/), Figma's release notes and help center, or your coding agent's changelog.
- **Monthly (2 h):** try one new capability on a real task and log it in your AI log.
- **Quarterly (half day):** review your policy, context and tool list. What's stale? What's new and proven?
- **Yearly:** re-run your context experiments with the latest models. NN/G warns that *"a curation decision made for today's models may be wrong for next year's."*

**Filter for durability.** Ask of any new trend:

1. Does it solve a real problem we have?
2. Is it based on an **open standard** or a proprietary lock-in?
3. Do practitioners report sustained use, or just launch-day excitement?
4. Does it strengthen or weaken human judgment, users in the loop and system consistency?

If it doesn't pass at least three of the four, wait.

## Congratulations

You've gone from "Figma designer who uses AI sometimes" to someone who can:

- explain how AI works and where it fails,
- use it with judgment across the whole design process,
- build a system that steers **everyone's** AI toward your standards,
- connect design to code through agents,
- define how designers and engineers collaborate in this new reality,
- and lead a team through the change with evidence and empathy.

That is what an AI-native design lead is. The tools will change; these capabilities won't.
